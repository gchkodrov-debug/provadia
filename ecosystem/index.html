The Roll of Houses

Chronological, Numbered, Unranked

The roll is kept in the order houses are born — numbered, chronological, and nothing more. There are no scores on this page, no ranks, and no ordering by performance — ever. A house's place on the roll is the day it was born, and that never changes.

    The roll is empty. When the first house is born, its entry will stand here carrying its registry number, its name — a name of its own, never containing PROVADIA — its birth receipt from the Day-Book, and its standing: STANDING, DORMANT, FALLEN, STRUCK, SUSPENDED or DEPARTED, as the law of the house provides. Beside every entry stand two lines, the whole of what a house may claim. The Mark's one fixed line:

    A house of the Town of PROVADIA — recorded at birth, built by the town, vouched for by no one

    And beside the mark, the honesty sentence, word for word:

    PROVADIA is a place, not an investment. Holding it names you a Bricklayer in the town registry and gives you a vote on the Wall’s fate — and nothing else: no redemption, no yield, no rights, no expected return.

    Nothing is deleted from this record. A house that falls, or is struck, stays on the roll with its receipts beside it — the roll remembers what the street would rather forget.
