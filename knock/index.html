The Knock · The Door

The Door Itself

This is a founder's door — a door you knock on.

It is not a signup. There is no form behind it, and no form on this page. It is not a queue you buy into: no place in any line is sold, held, or promised here. It is not a promise: a knock, when knocking opens, promises nothing to anyone.

Behind the door stands a judgment, made by the insiders of the town, on evidence — and the criteria below, published before the gate, are the whole shape of that evidence. A founder can read everything on this page and knock on nothing yet: the gate state at the foot of the page is the town's present truth.

The Knock · The Criteria

What "Strong" Means

The door asks for two proofs, stated plainly, in the founder's own terms. Both are judged on evidence a founder brings — never on anything he holds.

A strong idea — a real story: what the coin is, who it is for, and why it should exist. A coin with no story has nothing to be judged.

A strong foundation — the ground a founder stands on: his own means to pay his own creation charges from his own wallet — the town never fronts money — the staying power to keep his own record, and a name he is willing to sign in public.

The application package — the founder comes with his documents ready: the idea in his own words, the foundation above, and the coin's image.

The Mint Mark

The image carries the family mint mark: the full name PROVADIA or the initial P somewhere on the coin image, so the coin is known on sight as a house of the family — the way mints have always signed their coins. The mark is attribution on the image only: the coin's name and ticker stay its own, and no Wall or gold imagery ever appears on a child's coin. An image submitted without the mark is declined, with the reason stated — every refusal carries its reason and its receipt.

What is never weighed

A PROVADIA balance. Holding the town's coin is neither required nor weighed — in any amount, for any duration. The qualifying act is a signature, not a purchase.

The Knock · The Answer

How a Knock Is Answered

The procedure is published before the doors open, and it does not change from knock to knock:

  1. 1

    A live conversation

    the founder and the town speak, in person, about the idea and the foundation

  2. 2

    The covenant read in full

    the whole covenant of the section below, read before anything is signed

  3. 3

    The signature

    the qualifying act — a signature against the disclosed covenant

  4. 4

    The decision

    acceptance or refusal, made by the insiders, on the evidence

Every acceptance and every refusal is receipted in the Day-Book. A refused knock is answered with its receipt, not with silence.

The public queue face shows the line in chronological order — names as the founders themselves give them; no scores, no ranks.

The queue is empty.

It is empty because no knock can be taken yet. When the gate opens and the first knock is answered, its receipt — acceptance or refusal — will stand here in its order.

The Knock · Disclosed at the Door

The Covenant, Readable Before Anyone Knocks

Nobody joins blind. The whole covenant stands here, in plain words, before the gate — read it before any knock, and hold the town to every word of it.

Where the money goes

Of a house's own creator fees: 80% to the founder · 10% to the owner personally · 10% to the community, stored in the vaults — into Vault 1's tithe book, the contract's own book, where it accumulates receipted until the town rules its use.

The tithe, and how it moves

The tithe is taken on creator fees only, and it moves by honor-forwarding: the founder forwards his house's shares himself — the town never touches a founder's wallet. Each house renders a monthly statement; its shares are forwarded within 7 days and receipted within 48 hours. The tithe is a share of flow, never a debt. There are no arrears, ever.

The package and the mark

The application package and the mint-mark requirement of the criteria above are part of this covenant: the idea in the founder's own words, his foundation, and the coin's image carrying the full name PROVADIA or the initial P. An image without the mark is declined, with the reason stated.

The consequences, in plain words

claim fees and miss your forwarding once → a notice · miss twice → a second notice · miss three times → suspended from the family until you forward the shares of what you claimed · deception or refusal → struck by the founder's ruling, published with evidence

That is the Graduated Door — the road before the strike.

The covenant's pillars

The founder is sovereign — his wallet, his signature, his costs. The town never holds his funds, never co-signs, and never appears on his coin's controls.

The Mark — a house may claim one fixed line, and nothing beyond it: “A house of the Town of PROVADIA — recorded at birth, built by the town, vouched for by no one” — set beside its registry number and the honesty sentence. Nothing more may be claimed, in any words.

The Promotion Clause — the town never organizes or ranks a founder's promotion. A house's promotion is its founder's own work, as the town's is the town's.

The Strike Procedure — a documented breach earns a private notice with hash-logged evidence; the house's founder has 14 days to answer; the town's founder rules alone; and the ruling is published with its evidence.

Death & Departure — a house silent for 60 days stands dormant; at 90 days it stands fallen. An unannounced transfer of the creator wallet is an automatic strike.

Capacity — at most 2 house starts per month, and at most 5 active houses.

No Duty — the town owes a house nothing. A house's losses are its own.

The Child's Whole Duty — the covenant, the tithe, and its own record — nothing more is owed, and nothing more is asked.

The Honesty Sentence

PROVADIA is a place, not an investment. Holding it names you a Bricklayer in the town registry and gives you a vote on the Wall’s fate — and nothing else: no redemption, no yield, no rights, no expected return.
