Epoch 01

Provadia–Solnitsata — The Salt Town

~5500–4200 BC · above Provadia, Bulgaria

Proven Archaeology

  • Among Europe’s oldest known prehistoric salt-production centres.
  • A fortified salt-making settlement above Provadia, Bulgaria.
  • Salt was baked into bricks.

Scholarly Interpretation

  • Professor Vassil Nikolov’s reading: the salt cakes served as early prestige and exchange value — in his words, salt was money, and Provadia was Europe’s first mint.
  • He admits, in his own words on the record, that there is no direct evidence for how the trade worked. This town tells his reading as a reading — never as settled fact.

Epoch 02

The Varna Necropolis — The Gold

~4560–4450 BC · found October 1972

Proven Archaeology

  • Radiocarbon dates the cemetery to about 4560–4450 BC.
  • More than 3,000 gold objects, over 6 kg — among the oldest worked gold in human history.
  • Found by accident in October 1972 by excavator operator Raycho Marinov.
  • Grave 43 held a sceptre — a stone axe on a gold-sheathed shaft — buried with a man scholarship calls most likely a chief and priest. Not a proven king; this town does not crown him.
