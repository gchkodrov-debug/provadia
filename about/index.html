Face One · The Place

The Place

PROVADIA is a town before it is a token.

It is named for Provadia–Solnitsata — the salt town above Provadia, among the oldest settlements in Europe — where salt was baked into bricks and, in Professor Nikolov's reading of the evidence, carried as value. The town on this site is a rebuilding of that place: its streets, its Wall, and its book.

The archaeology under the name is proven, and it is told here in full. What the ground has given up:

Found by accident — In October 1972 an excavator operator, Raycho Marinov, struck gold in Varna's industrial zone and carried a shoebox of it to the museum.

The dating — Radiocarbon puts the Varna cemetery in use about 4560–4450 BC.

The gold — More than 3,000 objects, over 6 kg of gold — among them the sceptre of Grave 43, a stone axe on a gold-sheathed shaft, buried with the man the museum calls most likely a chief and priest.

Interpretation — Not Settled Fact

Professor Vasil Nikolov, the salt town's excavator, holds that salt was money — that the salt brick was currency and Provadia Europe's first mint, and that salt wealth explains Varna's gold. He admits there is no direct evidence for how the trade worked; other scholars call 'Europe's oldest town' hyperbole. In this town we tell it as what it is: one professor's reading of the ground.

The threshold stone of this page

PROVADIA is a place, not an investment. Holding it names you a Bricklayer in the town registry and gives you a vote on the Wall’s fate — and nothing else: no redemption, no yield, no rights, no expected return.
